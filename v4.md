## Research: best practices for interactive dev-resume sites

- **Mobile is the first read, not an afterthought.** ~68% of initial
  portfolio views happen on a phone, and hiring managers often screen
  candidates from their phone between other tasks — so mobile isn't a
  secondary breakpoint, it's the primary one to design contrast/legibility
  for. ([Developer Portfolio Guide 2026, Hakia](https://hakia.com/skills/building-portfolio/))
- **Single-page density beats multi-page depth for a resume site
  specifically.** Recruiters spend roughly 30-60 seconds on a first pass;
  a single-page layout puts the whole pitch in one continuous scroll with
  no back-and-forth, while multi-page structure is better suited to
  content that genuinely needs its own URL (blog posts, case studies) or
  scales over time. This matches the "subroutes aren't required" note —
  the fix is making the one-page pitch more complete, not necessarily
  deleting the deeper pages. ([Single-Page vs. Multi-Page Web Design, UXPin](https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/single-page-vs-multi-page-ui-design-pros-cons/))
- **3D/scroll effects should degrade gracefully, not just look good.**
  Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, keep a text/keyboard-accessible
  fallback for anything WebGL-driven, and don't animate everything at
  once — pick a few key moments. ([W3C Mobile Accessibility Best
  Practices](https://www.w3.org/WAI/GL/mobile-a11y-tf/wiki/Mobile_A11y_Best_Practices))
- **Mobile 3D needs an explicit performance budget.** Lower polygon
  counts, fewer simultaneous animated elements, and CSS transform/opacity
  animations (not layout-triggering properties) keep scroll-linked scenes
  smooth on older/weaker devices. ([3D Web Design Best Practices, Verox
  Studio](https://www.veroxstudio.com/blog/3d-web-design-best-practices-a-complete-guide-to-immersive-web-experiences-in-2))
- **Substance over volume in the project showcase.** Employers
  consistently say they'd rather see 3-5 polished, working pieces than a
  long list of shallow ones — reinforces keeping the persona pages
  focused rather than sprawling. ([Developer Portfolio Guide 2026,
  Hakia](https://hakia.com/skills/building-portfolio/))

**Open follow-ups this doesn't answer yet:** how our specific 3D-heavy
`fixed`-canvas + gradient-scrim approach compares to a plain CSS/DOM
fallback on low-end mobile GPUs, and whether the site should offer a
`prefers-reduced-motion` fallback that freezes the 3D scenes entirely.
